# Biomass_yieldTables (development version)

* The yield-curve figure now colours species with the optional input `sppColorVect` (the named vector of species colours made by `Biomass_borealDataPrep`, as used by `Biomass_core` and the other LandR modules), so a species keeps the same colour in every module and study area. Without it, ggplot's default colours are used.
* New parameters `headstartYears` (default 5; 0 = off) and `headstartRegen` (default `c("serotiny", "resprout")`) and new output `yieldTablesCumulativeHeadstart`: species whose `postfireregen` is in `headstartRegen` grow alone for `headstartYears` before the others are added at age 1 with `initialB`, as after a fire. `age` is the age of the first cohorts, so the late species have no rows before `headstartYears` + 1 and there is no age-0 row; it is not meant for CBM. Only `yieldTableIndex`es with such a species and at least two species are rerun; the others are copied from `yieldTablesCumulative`. The two simulation phases are illustrative only: their cohortData files are written to the session's `tempdir()` and deleted as soon as the tables are read, and they are not cached.
* The yield-curve figure now shows the most prevalent yield tables (most pixels) within the most prevalent `ecoregionGroup`s, instead of random ones, with panels labelled `<ecoregionGroup>--pg<yieldTableIndex>`. New parameter `numEcoregionGroups` (default 4); `numPlots` is the total number of curves, split among them.
* Three figures are now saved. In all of them, each panel is one "yield table": a stand grown from bare ground, with the species it contains competing for space, and a line per species showing its biomass (g/m2) against stand age. They are only for checking by eye that the simulated species behave plausibly.
  1. *Yield Curves from the most prevalent plots*: the stands that cover the most pixels in the study area, shown for the most common ecoregion groups. It shows what the landscape mostly looks like.
  2. *Yield Curves with a head start*: after a fire, species that come back from seed banks (serotiny) or resprouts get a few years' lead over the others. Eight example stands are drawn twice, with all species starting together (left, "plain") and with the head start (right). A head start typically lets a pioneer species (e.g. lodgepole pine) establish before a shade-tolerant one (e.g. Engelmann spruce) can suppress it.
  3. *Species growing with competition*: 12 random stands with at least two species, chosen so that every species appears at least once. It shows how species grow when they have to compete rather than grow alone: typically shade-intolerant species peak early and shade-tolerant ones take over later, while suppressed species stay near zero until the dominant one dies.
* New parameter `initialB` (default 10, as in Biomass_core). Every yield table now has biomass 0 at age 0 and `initialB` at age 1, then grows as a Biomass_core cohort does; before, tables started from 1 g/m2 at age 0, which let rounding decide the winner in species mixes. The age-0 row stays, so LandRCBM_split3pools and CBM_core are unaffected, and the 0-10 age relabelling is gone.
* Reverted the start of yield-table cohorts at age 1 and `initialB` (#24): tables again start at age 0 with B = 1, which LandRCBM_split3pools and CBM_core rely on (an age-0 row per table, increments from age 0). New tests state what consumers rely on: every table starts at age 0 with biomass <= 1 g/m2, and ages run 0, 1, 2, ... without gaps or repeats.
* `reqdPkgs` now lists `crayon`, `digest`, `ggplot2`, `reproducible` and `SpaDES.project`, which the module's code uses.
